Names used here

Hermes
An open-source personal AI agent from Nous Research that runs in chat apps and the terminal, and on a schedule. It is the always-on base the rest sits on.
ai-os
Author's thin personal layer on top of Hermes: a daily project board, an idea inbox, a ledger that logs every task, and a queue for coding jobs.
AEGIS
The assistant the author runs locally on small models, with the harness around it: the code that decides what a model can do and checks what it claims. ai-os borrows its guard, ledger and completion-gate patterns.
Claim-gate
A small checker that reads an agent's answer before the turn ends. If a claim has no tool call behind it, the agent is sent back once to check. No model does the judging.

Lesson numbers follow the ten lessons in the research note of 2026-10-26; every one of them appears in at least one stage. The dose figures come from Griffiths and colleagues, 2011, who tested 0, 5, 10, 20 and 30 mg per 70 kg in 18 volunteers: complete mystical experience 0, 5.6, 11.1, 44.4, 55.6%; extreme fear in 0, 0, 0, 1 and 6 volunteers. Every link here is an analogy, and the research is about people, not language models. Figures were checked against the papers on 2026-11-05. The protocol copies the shape of the clinical method, not the drug.
